The problem LintelDesk tries to solve

Most modern productivity tools are cloud-first: a document editor, a spreadsheet, a task board, and a diagramming tool, each a separate subscription, each needing an internet connection, each storing your data on someone else's server.

LintelDesk combines four of those functions (Docs, Sheets, Tasks, and Canvas) in one native Windows program that runs on your machine, keeps files in open formats on your own hard drive, and is paid for once. It will not fit every workflow. Teams that need real-time multi-user collaboration or mobile access are better served by a cloud suite. LintelDesk is for people who work mainly on one Windows PC and want their files to stay there.

How LintelDesk handles your data

What the application does and does not do with your files.

STORAGE

Local files only

Workspaces are saved to your hard drive in the .linteldesk format, with open formats such as .md, .csv, .json, and .png for documents and exports.

NETWORK

No account required

You never create an account or sign in to open and edit files. The internet is used only at purchase and download, not during day-to-day use.

TELEMETRY

No usage tracking

The application does not send usage analytics or document content to any server. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

BACKUPS

You own your backups

Files live only on your machine, so there is no cloud backup. Include your workspace folder in the backup routine you already use.

Why sales are final

LintelDesk does not offer refunds, and the reason is technical rather than a way to avoid responsibility. Here is the reasoning in full.

LintelDesk is a one-time purchase with no license keys, no accounts, and no online activation. The installer is a complete, working copy of the software that runs fully offline. Once a customer has downloaded it, they keep a working copy permanently. There is no server that can switch it off, and no key I can cancel.

That means a refund could not take the software back. A buyer would receive their money and still own a fully working program. Because of that, I mark all sales as final instead of offering a refund process that cannot actually reverse the purchase. That is the only reason, and it is not an attempt to avoid refunds or to make problems hard to raise.

If something is wrong, you are not left on your own. Verify your download

Being open about the installer is part of being trustworthy.

The Windows installer for v1.0.0 is currently not code-signed. Because of this, Windows SmartScreen may show an "unrecognized app" warning when you run it. You can check that your download is the file published here by comparing its SHA-256 hash:

629EFBE41C3288341BAB67BAE7031F9BFC4416102FD9C1BED594A2C19C87B2EB

In PowerShell, run Get-FileHash "path\to\installer.exe" -Algorithm SHA256 and compare the result. A matching hash shows the file is unchanged since it was published; it does not on its own prove a file is safe, so you are welcome to scan the installer with your own security software before running it.

Why does Windows show a SmartScreen warning?

When you launch LintelDesk for the first time, Windows may display a message such as "Windows protected your PC" or identify the publisher as unknown. This can happen because the v1.0.0 installer is not currently code-signed, so Microsoft SmartScreen may not have enough reputation information to recognize it.

This warning does not automatically mean the application is malicious, but it also does not guarantee that the file is safe. Before running the installer, verify its SHA-256 hash in the download verification section and scan the actual downloaded file using your own security software.

You can also review the LintelDesk VirusTotal URL report for additional information. Please note that a URL report is not necessarily a scan of the installer executable itself.

Only proceed if you have independently verified the download and trust its source. If your security software detects a specific threat, do not bypass the warning.

What happens to my files if I stop using LintelDesk?

They stay on your hard drive in open formats (Markdown, CSV, JSON, PNG). Uninstalling does not delete or lock your documents.
